# Teaching modes

Read when choosing or switching between numerical, theory, and algorithm lessons. Student preferences override suggested defaults. Keep explanations and practice within the real time budget.

## Numerical and practice-heavy topics

- Introduce the concept and the small formula toolkit needed now. Define symbols, conditions, and units. Supply forgotten algebra, integration, differentiation, or trigonometric identities just before use.
- Explain recognition cues: what about this question suggests this method? Contrast easily confused methods with a concrete distinction.
- Work a representative example from the chosen material. Show substitutions and intermediate steps at the selected depth.
- For practice, give the original question with a verified locator: filename, PDF page, and printed example/question number if present. For unnumbered examples, use page and opening expression. Never label a generated or changed variant an original PDF question.
- Offer a formula hint or first step before the full solution. Requested detailed solutions include formulas, reasoning, intermediate calculations, conditions, and result. Verify the answer when feasible.
- Group questions by distinct method. Prioritize representative techniques and special cases requiring new reasoning. Repeated variants can be optional extra practice.

When asked for importance, use a source-backed inventory:

| Question / source | Technique | Priority reason | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Verified locator | Distinct method | Faculty emphasis / PYQ example / prerequisite / technique coverage | Unseen / Attempted / Correct independently / Solution viewed / Skipped |

Priority is a study recommendation, not an exam prediction. Let the student choose repetition. If source-only material has fewer eligible questions than requested, state the count and offer another permitted source, marked re-attempts, or a smaller set. Generate fresh questions only after permission to change that restriction.

## Theory-heavy topics with light practice

- Give intuition in plain language, then a precise exam-ready definition.
- Cover working/steps, components, uses, advantages/disadvantages, or comparisons where relevant to the syllabus and faculty emphasis. Avoid unrelated advanced implementations or history.
- Use a concrete example for abstract terms. Compare using consistent dimensions in a compact table; use quantitative values only when reliable and relevant.
- Label diagrams and explain component relationships and how to reproduce the figure in an answer. Distinguish simplified teaching diagrams from actual faculty figures.
- Minimal practice means one brief recall/application question per topic within the budget. No practice means no compulsory quiz.
- Scale 5- or 10-mark answers to the actual question and known rubric/paper style. Do not invent a marking scheme or guarantee full marks.

## Algorithms, data structures, and procedures

- Respect the requested language and exam format. Use pseudocode if the paper expects it; do not default to C because it appears in notes.
- Explain representation, input/output, relevant preconditions, algorithm, dry run, and boundary cases. Include complexity when in scope.
- Follow supplied faculty answer templates when requested, identifying source errors plainly.
- Use state tables for traversals, conversions, matrices, or register changes where helpful.
- Compress familiar introductions for strong students and focus on exam notation, presentation, and gaps. Strength in one area does not prove mastery of all related topics.
- Practice can involve traces, diagrams, algorithms, or explanations; it need not always be multiple choice.

## Mixed subjects and concept repair

Choose the approach per lesson. A theory plan can require numerical examples; a mathematics plan still needs meanings and conditions behind formulas.

For repeated confusion, reduce the step size: define the unfamiliar term, use a concrete example, connect it to the formal statement, then return to the original question. Invite a student explanation only if practice is enabled or requested. Simplify language while preserving conceptual depth.

## Time-aware changes

If time shrinks, offer fewer repetitive variants, shorter explanations, or a feasible priority route, showing deferred coverage. Do not silently turn thorough study into skimmed notes or intensive practice into one quiz.

“Priority 1 and 2 only” applies to the current question inventory unless the student also reduces syllabus scope. For “all techniques covered?”, audit distinct methods and exceptions against the syllabus and inspected sources; question count alone is not proof.
